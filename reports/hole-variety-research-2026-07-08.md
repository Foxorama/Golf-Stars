# Research: what makes a golf hole hard vs. interesting — for the generator (2026-07-08)

Background for **GS-variety-3** (variety at mid-to-high difficulty). Complaint: at higher
difficulty — especially Rainbow Road, Void and Cetus — every hole becomes "an exceptionally
long bendy hole that all look exactly the same." Root cause: the generator scales **length ↑
and bend ↑** on *every* hole as wildness rises; those are the two levers that read as monotonous
in aggregate.

## The three schools
- **Penal** — one correct shot, errors punished (engages the body).
- **Strategic** — multiple routes, you pick your own risk (engages the mind).
- **Heroic** — carry-or-bail-out, severe penalty but big reward (engages the heart).
Good design leans strategic/heroic. MacKenzie's throughline: **difficulty should come from
decisions and angles, not just length and bend**; "infinite variety in the strokes required,"
"every hole a different character," and always leave the weaker player an alternate route at the
cost of a fraction of a stroke.

## A) Difficulty levers (→ 2D corridor/green/hazard mapping)
- **Length within par** — crudest lever; use sparingly, one axis among many.
- **Forced carries, position matters** — off the tee (nerve + bail-out), mid-fairway (club/placement
  decision), at the approach (aerial stop shot). Vary the crossing's *distance-from-tee*.
- **Diagonal hazards** — angled edge across the line: choose how much to bite off (the Cape idea).
- **Staggered / cross bunkering** — offset hazards down alternating sides so no line is safe for every club.
- **Narrowing / pinch points** — corridor tightens at the *expected landing distance* (neck/hourglass).
- **Green size & firmness** — small + firm greens reject imprecise approaches (dominant modern lever).
- **Tucked pins behind hazards** — push the pin toward the hazard-guarded lobe; attack vs fat-of-green.
- **False fronts / run-offs** — front repels short shots; sides feed to recovery.
- **Wind** — changes effective length; reads true off the shot bearing.
- **Doglegs (severity & direction)** — one lever; overuse is exactly the monotony trap here — cap frequency.
- **Split / alternate fairways** — short-dangerous vs long-safe (heroic optionality).
- **Central hazard** — obstacle mid-fairway forks the hole; each side a different follow-on angle.
- **Angle-of-attack demands** — green defended so only one fairway side opens the pin; challenge is placement.

## B) Variety / interest principles
- **Mix of pars + half-par holes** — a 4½ (long 4 / reachable 5) makes the most tension.
- **Length variety WITHIN each par** — short/mid/long par 3s, drivable/standard/long par 4s,
  reachable/three-shot par 5s. The single biggest anti-monotony lever.
- **Shape variety — NOT every hole a dogleg** — straight holes are interesting when defended by
  bunkering + green contour + angle demands. Doglegs monotonous in aggregate.
- **Template holes as set-pieces** — Redan/Cape/Biarritz/Short/… a vocabulary of distinct identities.
- **Risk-reward optionality** — memorable holes offer two routes with near-equal expected score
  (the "~60% go for it" band). Obvious correct play = forgettable.
- **Alternate dogleg direction & rhythm** — don't bend every hole the same way; space hard holes.
- **A hard hole need not be long or bent** — a short par 4 / mid par 3 to a firm, tucked green can be
  the hardest hole on the course.

## C) Classic templates (name — idea — 2D expression)
- **Redan** — angled/tilted green, deep front-short bunker; run it in off the slope. → green tilted along
  a diagonal axis, hazard short-side, feed-slope on the open side.
- **Cape** — diagonal carry, bite off as much as you dare. → diagonal penalty edge, carryable width varies.
- **Biarritz** — long 3, huge green split by a transverse swale. → oversized green + transverse contour valley.
- **Road Hole** — angled tee carry, green knifed between front bunker & back trouble. → dogleg corner carry
  + green pinched front-bunker/back-penalty.
- **Short** — very short 3, small wildly-contoured ringed green. → short corridor, small green, strong contour.
- **Punchbowl** — bowl gathers to centre (a relief/rhythm hole). → green-contour bowl (raised rim, low centre).
- **Alps** — blind carry over a ridge to a hidden green + cross-bunker beyond. → cross-hazard band to fully carry.
- **Double Plateau** — two raised tiers; reach the *right* one. → green split into two contour lobes + valley.
- **Eden** — small firm back-to-front green, deep front bunker + back hazard. → narrow safe depth band.
- **Hog's Back / spine** — crowned centreline sheds off-line shots. → raised centreline: on-spine releases, off-spine rolls off.

## D) Top recommendations for THIS generator
1. **Sample length as a distribution WITHIN each par, not a wildness-scaled value.** Guarantee short/
   drivable holes still appear deep in — wildness shifts the *mix*, never removes the short end.
2. **Cap the length+bend share of difficulty** — spend the rest on greens/hazards/angles.
3. **Make short/firm-green holes a first-class HARD archetype** (the Short/Eden path). Hard ≠ long.
4. **Anti-repeat scheduler** — bias each hole away from the previous hole's (length-class, shape,
   dogleg-direction). Allow straight holes at max difficulty. Alternate dogleg directions.
5. **Angle-of-attack** — couple pin-side with fairway-side so tee-shot placement matters.
6. **Genuine two-route optionality on the interesting holes** (short-dangerous vs long-safe).
7. **Named template set-pieces** parameterized by terrain.
8. **Move difficulty into greens** — size, firmness, tucked pins, false fronts, contour.
