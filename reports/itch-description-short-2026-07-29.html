Space golf, but make it an RPG. Fly a galaxy of procedurally-generated golf courses, earn your bag, upgrade your ship, and find out what has been sleeping at the root of the World-Tree.

Every course is grown from a seed — real doglegs, real bunkering, greens with contour you have to read. Fifteen worlds, each with its own physics: links in the ice rings where the crosswind never rests, molten doglegs on the ember world, a star-ocean shelf with whales breaching under the cliffs, a derelict ship where you carom off bulkheads across gaps of open space. The wildness scales as you travel. The fairness doesn't — the game proves every hole is playable before you ever see it.

Four ways to play

  • 🌠 Story Tour — five chapters, five tournaments, five formats. Pick your partners, then pick a side when the Snake Cult comes calling.
  • 🚀 The Voyage — three arcs, three bosses, one win. Survive the cut or the tour drops you.
  • 🌌 Unending Universe — endless survival. How deep can you get?
  • 🗺 Star Tour — free-roam the chart, land anywhere, chase your course records.

Four golfers, and a caddy you'll regret

Feather fades it, Larry bombs it, Bo zips it back on a string, Huang-Woo fights a hook — all of it baked into the ball flight, not a stat bar. Then you hire a caddy, and hiring a new one fires the old one. Dr Chipinski holes out from off the green. The Mystic Mole finds a line nobody else can see. The Prognostic Parrot plays your shot twice and keeps the better one.

Golf that feels like golf

Pull back to load the swing and watch the spray cone open — the cone is the dispersion you're rolling against, drawn to scale. A driver runs out on firm turf; a wedge lands steep and checks. The ball skips, settles, and trickles down the fall line. Every run is reproducible from its seed.

No accounts. No tracking. No nonsense.

The Far Carry collects nothing and sends nothing — no sign-up, no analytics, no ads, no cookies. Your save lives on your device, and you can export the whole thing to a file. That isn't a promise in a policy; the build fails if it stops being true.

Readable-text scaling, reduced motion, full keyboard play and screen-reader narration are all in the settings.
